01 / Operations & Data Integration

ProfitLens

A real-time profit analytics dashboard for restaurants selling across Deliveroo, Uber Eats and their own counter. It answers a question no single platform can: what did we actually earn on that order?

The problem

A restaurant takes orders from Deliveroo, Uber Eats, Just Eat and its own point of sale. Every platform has its own dashboard, its own commission structure, and its own way of presenting numbers.

Revenue looks healthy on every one of those screens. But nobody knows real profit until the end of the month, when the platform invoices arrive and the commissions are finally subtracted. Until then, managers are guessing — and they are guessing about which dishes and which channels are actually worth selling.

The gap is structural. No platform can tell you your margin, because no platform knows your food costs, and none of them know what the others charge you.

What it does

ProfitLens connects to HubRise, a middleware layer that sits between point-of-sale systems and delivery platforms and unifies every order stream into a single event feed. ProfitLens subscribes to that feed by webhook and computes true profit on each order as it arrives.

  • Receives orders in real time and calculates commission at the moment of ingestion
  • Matches every line item against the product catalogue by SKU to attach food costs
  • Shows KPIs, revenue trends, per-platform and per-category margins, and an hourly heatmap
  • Generates daily reports with manager notes, email delivery and CSV export
  • Flags data quality problems — unknown platforms, unmatched items — in a pipeline monitor

Architecture

The data model follows the medallion pattern from data engineering: three layers, each with one job.

Bronze

raw_webhooks

The unmodified HubRise payload, stored as JSONB, one row per webhook received regardless of outcome. Never modified after insert.

If parsing logic changes, the original event is still there to reprocess. An accountant who receives a bad invoice does not throw the original away.

Silver

orders + order_items

One row per accepted order, one per line item. Prices and costs are stored as a snapshot taken at the time of the order.

A carbonara sold in April at £17.00 with a £4.76 cost must still report those figures in April, even after the menu is repriced in May.

Gold

SQL functions

Parameterised PostgreSQL functions, called over RPC, that return pre-aggregated rows filtered by date, platform and category.

The dashboard never pulls raw order rows. It asks a question and gets an answer back.

The bug that shaped the architecture

After seeding the database with 3,000 orders, the dashboard reported 1,000 orders and roughly a third of the expected revenue. No error. No warning. No failed request.

The cause was PostgREST, which caps every .select() at 1,000 rows by default as a denial-of-service safeguard. The dashboard was fetching rows and summing them in JavaScript, so 2,000 orders were being dropped silently — and the numbers on screen were confidently wrong.

I considered three fixes:

  1. Raise the cap with .range(0, 9999) — a magic number that breaks again at 10,001 rows and ships the entire table over the wire on every page load.
  2. Paginate in a loop — several round trips, and the same problem simply relocated to a higher threshold.
  3. Move aggregation into the database — a SQL SUM returns one row no matter how large the table grows.

I took the third. Views first, then parameterised functions once date filtering was needed, since views cannot take arguments.

Aggregation belongs where the data lives. Postgres was built to do SUM, COUNT and GROUP BY; shipping rows to Node just to add them up is moving data uphill for nothing.

The webhook pipeline

Every order that arrives is written to the raw layer before anything is parsed, then walked through a sequence where each failure mode has a defined resting state rather than an exception.

  1. 01 Store the raw payload, untouched
  2. 02 Ignore update events; only creations proceed
  3. 03 Unwrap the HubRise new_state envelope
  4. 04 Validate — missing ID or total is rejected
  5. 05 Check idempotency — a repeat order ID is duplicate
  6. 06 Look up the platform — an unknown channel is held
  7. 07 Calculate commission and insert the order
  8. 08 Match each item by SKU and snapshot its cost

A duplicate returns 200, not an error, so that HubRise does not retry it. An unknown platform is held in the raw layer rather than discarded, so it can be resolved and reprocessed later. Nothing is ever lost because it arrived in an unexpected shape.

Decisions and trade-offs

Supabase over raw Postgres

Started on the raw pg client and migrated within the first sprint. Supabase brought authentication, row level security, and two clients at two privilege levels — so the service-role key is never exposed to the browser. The cost: PostgREST serialises NUMERIC as a JSON string to preserve decimal precision, so every numeric field crosses into TypeScript through Number(...).

Pure logic extracted to lib/

Business logic initially lived inside server components and route handlers, which import Next.js internals that Vitest cannot load. Pulling the pure functions into lib/ — files that import nothing from Next.js or Supabase — meant the tests and the application exercise the same function, not a copy of it. That separation is what made 125 unit tests possible.

Proportional commission allocation

Platform commission is distributed across an order's items by revenue share, following standard cost accounting practice, so per-product margins stay meaningful rather than arbitrary.

A read-only analytics layer

ProfitLens reads from HubRise and never writes back. The point of sale remains the source of truth for products and prices; ProfitLens enriches that data with commission rates and food costs to produce the one number no platform provides.

What it does not do

Knowing a system's limits matters as much as knowing its features. The significant ones:

  • No webhook authentication. There is no HMAC signature verification on the endpoint. Acceptable against the HubRise sandbox; a requirement before any production deployment.
  • Sandbox only. Validated against the HubRise sandbox and Order Injector, not against live Deliveroo or Uber Eats bridges.
  • Unmatched items skew margin. Items that cannot be matched to the catalogue are excluded from product breakdowns. Their revenue still counts toward totals but carries zero cost, which overstates profit.
  • Reports are live, not frozen. A daily report reflects current data rather than a snapshot locked at the close of that day.
